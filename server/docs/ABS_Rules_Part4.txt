AMERICAN BUREAU OF SHIPPING
RULES FOR BUILDING AND CLASSING STEEL VESSELS — 2024
PART 4 — VESSEL SYSTEMS AND MACHINERY
ABS — www.eagle.org

=============================================================================
CHAPTER 1 — CONDITIONS OF CLASSIFICATION
=============================================================================

1.1 Application
These Rules apply to steel vessels assigned ABS class notations. Part 4 covers propulsion shafting, auxiliary machinery, pressure vessels, piping systems, and electrical power systems.

1.2 Scope
All systems and components governed by Part 4 shall be designed, manufactured, installed, and tested in accordance with these Rules. Where International Standards (IEC, ASME, ISO) are referenced, they form part of these Rules.

1.3 Plan Approval
Drawings for the following shall be submitted for ABS approval: propulsion shafting; steering gear; pressure vessels > 0.5 m³ and > 1 bar; fuel oil, lube oil, and bilge piping schematics; electrical single-line diagrams; generator and switchboard data.

CHAPTER 2 — PROPULSION SHAFTING
=============================================================================

Section 1 — Shaft Diameters
2.1.1 Minimum diameter for solid propulsion shafts (ABS):
  Intermediate shaft: d = F × C × [(P_MCR × (1 + 0.01 × k)) / n]^(1/3) mm
  where:
    P_MCR = maximum continuous rated power (kW)
    n = shaft rotational speed (RPM)
    F = material factor = 100/σ_v^(1/3) where σ_v = min(160, 0.40 × UTS)
    C = type factor: 1.00 for intermediate shaft, 1.10 for thrust shaft, 0.95 for solid coupling
    k = age factor: 0 for ships ≤ 10 years, 5 for ships > 10 years.
2.1.2 Hollow shafts: Q = bore/outside diameter. Formula adjusted as d_hollow = d_solid / (1-Q^4)^(1/3). Maximum Q = 0.6.
2.1.3 Propeller shaft (tail shaft): d_tailshaft = 1.15 × d_intermediate (for oil-lubricated stern tube) or 1.20 × d_intermediate (for water-lubricated).

Section 2 — Shaft Couplings and Flanges
2.2.1 Flange coupling bolts: minimum 4 bolts, diameter per:
  d_bolt = 0.65 × d_shaft / n_bolts^(1/2) mm (fitted bolts, reamed hole).
  Fitted bolt material: yield ≥ 690 N/mm² for alloy steel, ≥ 450 N/mm² for carbon steel.
2.2.2 Flange thickness: minimum 0.20 × shaft diameter, not less than 20 mm.
2.2.3 Keyways: not permitted on intermediate or propulsion shafts > 300 RPM. Keyways permissible on shafts < 300 RPM with key fit per tolerance h6/H7.

Section 3 — Bearings and Stern Tubes
2.3.1 Stern tube bearing oil-lubricated: bearing length ≥ 3.5 × shaft diameter; aft seal type-approved.
2.3.2 Stern tube bearing water-lubricated (cutlass): bearing length ≥ 4.0 × shaft diameter; cooling water flow minimum 0.1 L/min per 25 mm bearing bore. Bronze or polymer bearing liner.
2.3.3 Intermediate shaft bearings: bearing length ≥ 2.5 × shaft diameter; load per bearing ≤ manufacturer rating at operating RPM. Alignment verified per UR M57.
2.3.4 Shaft earthing: earthing device (sliding contact with carbon brush or liquid metal) to prevent bearing current damage. Resistance < 100 Ω under normal operation.
2.3.5 Shaft vibration: continuous online shaft torsional and lateral vibration monitoring required for ships > 5000 GT from 2023 ABS notice.

Section 4 — Propeller
2.4.1 ABS recognizes NiBrAl (nickel-aluminium bronze) as standard material. Alternative: duplex stainless steel for high-erosion service.
2.4.2 Propeller blade design: ABS Rules Pt 4, Ch 2, Sec 2 / Appendix A. Stress at 0.35R not to exceed: bronze 110 N/mm² (steady), 90 N/mm² (dynamic); cast steel 125/105 N/mm².
2.4.3 Propeller-shaft fit: tapered bore with hydraulic nut. Taper ratio 1:10 to 1:15. Hydraulic push-up value per ABS table.
2.4.4 Propeller balance: static balance for all propellers ≤ 5 tonnes. Dynamic balance for propellers > 5 tonnes or shaft speed > 300 RPM.

CHAPTER 3 — AUXILIARY MACHINERY
=============================================================================

Section 1 — Pumps
3.1.1 All centrifugal pumps for critical services shall be of marine duty construction. Mechanical seal or packing gland. Casing: cast iron for seawater ≤ 250 kPa; bronze or duplex SS for higher pressure.
3.1.2 Pump foundations: vibration-isolated for pumps > 5 kW in accommodation-adjacent spaces. Flexible coupling between pump and motor.
3.1.3 Redundancy for essential pumps: cooling water, lube oil, fire main, bilge — minimum 2 pumps each capable of full service load. For ships > 500 GT.
3.1.4 Fire pump rated flow: per SOLAS Ch.II-2. Seawater fire pump: direct drive from diesel or from independent power source. Self-priming, capable of drawing up 4.5 m suction.
3.1.5 Bilge pump: self-priming. Pump total lift capacity minimum 6 m. See also bilge system requirements.

Section 2 — Compressors
3.2.1 Starting air compressors: minimum 2 (reciprocating or rotary screw). Capacity to refill main receivers from empty to full starting pressure in ≤ 1 hour.
3.2.2 Starting air receiver: minimum 2 receivers. Combined volume for 12 starting attempts (6 ahead, 6 astern) per SOLAS. Safety valve, moisture drain, pressure gauge on each receiver.
3.2.3 Instrument air: clean, dry instrument air at 7–8 bar for pneumatic instruments. Desiccant or refrigerated dryer, dew point < -20°C at line pressure.

Section 3 — Heat Exchangers
3.3.1 Central freshwater cooler: titanium or Ni-Resist cast iron or 90/10 CuNi plates for seawater side. Cooling capacity + 20% margin for fouled sea chest condition.
3.3.2 Fuel oil heater: steam or HTW heater with thermostat control. Bypass valve for manual override. Temperature sensor in HFO service line controlling heater steam valve.
3.3.3 Lube oil cooler: two independent lube oil coolers with 3-way valve for changeover during operation. Cooler pressure: lube oil side > sea water side to prevent seawater contamination.

Section 4 — Emergency Bilge Pumping from Engine Room
3.4.1 Direct bilge suction from largest installed pump (fire pump or ballast pump). Emergency suction diameter minimum 100 mm. Valve operated from engine room floor plate level and above.
3.4.2 Two independent power sources for bilge pumping (main and emergency power).

CHAPTER 4 — PRESSURE VESSELS AND BOILERS
=============================================================================

Section 1 — Pressure Vessels
4.1.1 All pressure vessels shall be designed, fabricated, and tested per ABS Rules or ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code (BPVC) Section VIII Division 1, unless otherwise specified.
4.1.2 Vessels accepted under ASME VIII/1: U-stamp on nameplate. ABS surveyor may witness final hydrostatic test as minimum survey requirement.
4.1.3 Where ABS rules apply directly (Class I vessels): design review, material review, and inspection at manufacturer by ABS surveyor.
4.1.4 Hydrostatic test: 1.5 × maximum allowable working pressure (MAWP) for all pressure vessels. Witnessed by ABS or ASME inspector.
4.1.5 Safety valves: sized for worst-case overpressure scenario. Capacity ≥ all connected heat sources + controlled leak-in rates. Fire case: re-rating per API 520/521 if applicable.
4.1.6 Relief valve set pressure ≤ MAWP. Maximum accumulation 10% above MAWP (ASME VIII, UG-125).
4.1.7 Pressure gauges: each pressure vessel to have a gauge with range 1.5–2.0 × MAWP. Gauge isolation valve. Gauge tested and calibrated annually.

Section 2 — Waste Heat Recovery (WHR) Systems
4.2.1 Exhaust gas economisers (EGE/EGB): designed per ABS rules for steam generation from exhaust. Sootblower required. Bypass damper to allow bypass during cleaning or low-load operation.
4.2.2 Steam drum: water level gauges (2), high/low alarm, safety valve, NRV on feed water.
4.2.3 EGE constructed per ASME Section I (Power Boilers) or equivalent standard accepted by ABS.

Section 3 — Boilers
4.3.1 Main boilers on steam ships: ABS Rules Part 4, Chapter 4 or ASME Section I.
4.3.2 Safety valve capacity: total capacity of all safety valves ≥ maximum steam generation rate of boiler at design conditions.
4.3.3 Safety valve discharge: directed to atmosphere via vent pipe. No valve between safety valve and boiler drum.
4.3.4 Feed water: chemical treatment to prevent scaling and corrosion. pH 9.5–11.0. Hardness < 0.1 ppm. Oxygen < 0.02 ppm.
4.3.5 Boiler access: manholes and mudhole doors. Minimum inspection frequency: internal every 2.5 years, external annually.

CHAPTER 5 — PIPING SYSTEMS
=============================================================================

Section 1 — General Piping Requirements
5.1.1 Piping system design pressure (DP): maximum service pressure plus 10% or 0.7 bar (whichever greater).
5.1.2 Wall thickness calculation:
  t = (P × d_o) / (2 × σ_allow × η + P) + c mm
  where P = DP (bar), d_o = outside diameter (mm), σ_allow = allowable stress (N/mm²), η = weld efficiency (1.0 for seamless; 0.85 for seam-welded), c = corrosion allowance (1.0 mm minimum; 2.0 mm for seawater).
5.1.3 Schedule minimum: SCH 40 for bore ≤ DN 50; SCH STD for DN 50–150; calculated wall for DN > 150.
5.1.4 Pipe joints: butt-welded joints for Class I piping. Socket-weld or flanged for DN ≤ 50. Flanged at equipment connections. Compression fittings not acceptable for Class I.
5.1.5 Flanges: ASME B16.5 or equivalent. Flange rating per system DP and temperature class.

Section 2 — Fuel Oil Service System
5.2.1 Fuel oil service tank: daily service tank of 8-hour capacity at MCR. Separate tanks for HFO and diesel.
5.2.2 Heating coils: in HFO tanks. Heating capacity to maintain HFO at minimum viscosity for injection (typically 50 cSt at injection point). Steam or HTW coils.
5.2.3 Drain cock: at lowest point of HFO tanks. Drained daily in port condition.
5.2.4 Overflow pipe: gravity return to settling tank. Overflow of service tank not to atmosphere.
5.2.5 Remote shut-off: quick-closing valve on each fuel tank suction operable from outside machinery space.
5.2.6 Low-sulphur fuel changeover: procedure in SEEMP for changing from HFO to LSFO in SOx ECAs. Changeover log required.

Section 3 — Lube Oil System
5.3.1 Engine lube oil: full-flow filter (duplex, changeable under pressure). Bypass alarm. Maximum pressure drop at clean element: 0.5 bar.
5.3.2 Centrifugal separator (purifier): for all main diesel engines. Capacity: minimum 20% of main engine lube oil system volume per hour.
5.3.3 Lube oil sump: gravity drain from engine bearings to sump. Sump level alarm (high and low). No gravity drain from sump to bilge without automatic valve.
5.3.4 Lube oil pre-lubrication: pre-lube pump cycles before main engine start. Pressure ≥ 1.5 bar maintained for 5 minutes before start permit given to engine control.

Section 4 — Cooling Water System
5.4.1 HT cooling water (jacket water): thermostatic control maintaining engine jacket temperature at manufacturer's setpoint (typically 80–90°C).
5.4.2 LT cooling water (sea water circuit): separate circuit for charge air cooler, lube oil cooler, and gearbox oil cooler.
5.4.3 Freshwater generator (FWG): condenser/evaporator type using engine LT cooling water. Salinity of produced water: < 5 ppm. Salinity alarm.
5.4.4 Sea chest: see DNV document for design requirements. Anti-fouling treatment in sea chest (copper or electrochlorine dosing).

CHAPTER 6 — ELECTRICAL INSTALLATIONS
=============================================================================

Section 1 — Power Generation and Distribution
6.1.1 ABS electrical rules are aligned with IEC 60092 series. Where IEC 60092 and ABS text differ, the more stringent requirement applies.
6.1.2 Generator rating: all generators rated at 0.8 power factor, 50 Hz or 60 Hz (as specified). Ambient rating: 45°C ambient temperature for continuous rating (tropics).
6.1.3 Essential consumers: list of essential services to be maintained in all conditions per SOLAS. Non-essential consumers: automatically disconnected on generator overload or blackout.
6.1.4 Preferential tripping: sequence of load shedding documented in maintenance manual. Priority 1 (kept connected): propulsion, steering, navigation, safety, bilge, fire. Priority 2 (first to shed): galley, accommodation HVAC, deck machinery.
6.1.5 Generator parallel operation: bus voltage dip on motor start ≤ 15% of nominal. Automatic synchroniser for ships with 3+ generators.

Section 2 — Emergency Power
6.2.1 Emergency generator: per SOLAS II-1 Reg 38 and ABS Rules.
6.2.2 Emergency switchboard: ABS requires that all connections between ESB and MSB include automatic transfer scheme, with manual override at both MSB and ESB.
6.2.3 Transitional power: battery bank capable of supplying emergency lighting and alarm for minimum 30 minutes before emergency generator comes online.
6.2.4 Emergency generator room: separate from main machinery space. Fire-rated boundaries (A-60). Independent ventilation. Access from above waterline.

Section 3 — Cathodic Protection
6.3.1 Hull cathodic protection: zinc or aluminium sacrificial anodes for underwater hull. Anode material and quantity: calculate based on impressed current density (0.1 A/m² average), exposed steel area, and design life (typically 5 years between drydocks).
6.3.2 ICCP: impressed current cathodic protection optional for large ships. System type-approved. Monitoring electrodes (silver/silver-chloride reference cells) in way of bow and amidships.
6.3.3 Bonding: all equipment connected to hull earth. Cathodic protection system bonding checked at each drydock.

CHAPTER 7 — SURVEYS AND RECORDS
=============================================================================

Section 1 — Required Surveys
7.1.1 Annual survey (AS): general condition of all machinery and systems. Function test of all safety devices. Check of all operating parameters against approved values.
7.1.2 Intermediate survey (IS) at 2.5 years: internal examination of boilers and pressure vessels; inspection of propulsion shafts (if interval extension not in effect); examination of steering gear.
7.1.3 Special survey (SS) at 5 years (renewal of Class): full examination including: propeller and shaft withdrawal (unless continuous survey programme in effect); pressure vessel internal; boiler internal; electrical insulation resistance testing.

Section 2 — Continuous Machinery Survey (CMS) Programme
7.2.1 ABS CMS allows machinery items to be examined at rolling 5-year intervals rather than all at special survey. Items eligible: main engine, generators, gearboxes, pumps, compressors, heat exchangers.
7.2.2 CMS records: all machinery examination records retained onboard and in ABS classification database.
7.2.3 Condition-based survey (CBS): vibration analysis and oil analysis programme substitutes for certain opening-up surveys. CBM (condition-based maintenance) accepted by ABS with supporting monitoring data.

Section 3 — Thickness Measurement
7.3.1 Structural thickness measurements required at each special survey after 10 years of age.
7.3.2 Regions of concern: bottom shell in way of bilge and keel; side shell in way of waterline; deck plating; inner bottom; hold frames.
7.3.3 Wastage allowance: structural members with measured thickness < (original thickness - 20% original thickness) require renewal or insert.
